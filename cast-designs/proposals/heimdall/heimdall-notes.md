# Heimdall: SVG proposal notes, round 1

## Canon comes first

Heimdall already has a design. The established sheet is at
`F:\Projects\gamecult-site\GameCult\static\interactive\cotsc-praxis\character-sheets\heimdall-sheet.png`,
with sprites under `sprites\heimdall\`. It shows dark skin, messy dark hair lit
gold, glowing gold eyes, a stern face, a navy high-collared coat with cobalt
lining and gold trim, a gold key pendant, golden keys, a glowing golden gate, and
a bridge he guards. The brief's three directions (horn, white-haired key halo,
finned helmet) invented a new character. They were dropped as reinventions. A, B
and C are now three takes on the canon design. They share one head and one coat,
and each puts weight on a different hook: the horn, the key, the gate.

Superseded sheets, kept for reference:
- `heimdall-old-A.svg`/`.png`: the brief's Horn Bearer, with spiky violet-black hair, a fur ruff and an ivory horn. Finished.
- `heimdall-old-B.svg`/`.png`: the brief's Keyring Halo, with white hair and an iron ring of twelve keys. A first-pass draft that was never polished. The hand reads as a peace sign.

Bifrost: the operator said "Heimdall guards Bifrost too". Each direction carries
a thin prismatic line, kept under 10 percent of the figure, so it never turns
into a rainbow costume. On A it runs along the horn's outer arc, on B along the
inside of the key's bow, and on C along the inner edge of the gate arch.

Shared canon read: palette is cobalt `#3466ff` (lining, key colour), navy
`#1b2452` (coat), line `#0d1a4d`, gold `#d8a73a` (eyes, trim, keys), skin
`#5a3a2a`, hair `#2e211c`. Eyes are sharp almonds with a gold iris, a dark upper
band, a bright lower crescent and two highlights. A key-shaped gold earring sits
on the near ear. Gold-brown slivers in the hair stand in for the canon's
gold-lit strands. A cobalt rim light runs on the side away from the light.

## A: The Horn Bearer (expression: On watch)

- The design puts an ivory Gjallarhorn with gold bands over his head as a crescent, from his far shoulder to above the near ear, with a strap across the chest. The face is level and neutral.
- Changes after looking: the first horn drew as a shepherd's crook, with a vertical bar and a hook. I rebuilt it as a true crescent whose bell ends above the near ear. I scaled the bust down so the arc reads, clipped the prismatic line and rim light inside the horn, and turned the hair highlights from strokes, which read as stripes, into tapered slivers.
- Silhouette: the strongest of the three. At 48 px the crescent alone names him.
- Round 2: ivory is the one colour not in the canon. Try the horn in navy and gold so the palette stays canon, and check that the silhouette still carries it. The strap still reads a little like a staff.

## B: The Great Key (expression: Granted)

- The design follows the canon's raised-key pose. A gold key taller than his head is held upright beside his face, toward the text box. It has a crowned bow (three spikes) and a diamond ward, and a gloved fist grips it below the bit. He gives a small smile with a gold tooth glinting, the only direction that uses the myth's golden teeth.
- Changes after looking: at first the fist covered the bit, so the key read as a sceptre. I extended the shaft so the hand grips below the bit, which now shows clearly above the fist. I also scaled the key up 15 percent because the 48 px silhouette was too thin.
- Silhouette: head plus a ringed staff. It reads at 128 px. At 48 px it is the weakest of the three, because the key is still a thin line.
- Round 2: make the bit bigger and the bow wider, or tilt the key 20 degrees so it crosses the frame. The fist is a blob, so draw clear knuckles.

## C: The Gate Warden (expression: Revoked)

- The design sets a gold gate arch behind him, with a keyhole medallion at the crown, faint circuit tracery and spired navy pillars with cobalt gems on both sides. A gloved palm is raised toward the text box. The brows and lids are pulled down into a hard frown.
- Changes after looking: a pale glow band inside the arch read as a second, muddy frame and greyed the silhouette, so I removed it. The brows disappeared into the dark fringe, so the revoked look now comes from slanted lowered lids. I also dropped a stray gold dot from the palm.
- Silhouette: an arched doorway with two spires, a figure inside and a raised hand. It is nameable at 48 px, but the gate is the read, not him.
- Round 2: the gate is scenery more than a worn hook. Test a version where the arch is smaller and sits as a halo-crown just behind the head, so that the character, not the backdrop, carries the shape. The full-body gate is cramped, so give it its own width.

## Strongest

A has the best silhouette and C shows the canon best. A canon-palette horn
(round 2 on A) is the likely winner. B's gold-tooth smile should carry into the
portrait set's "Granted" line whichever direction wins.

Sheets are self-contained SVG, built from fragments (one shared head and coat,
plus one hook file per direction) by a small assembler in the session scratchpad.
The SVGs are the deliverable. Edit them directly in round 2.
